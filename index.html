The water

Filtered by stone for 9,500 years

Carlsbad Alkaline Water is not filtered municipal water. Rain that falls on the Cleveland National Forest works its way through the rock for thousands of years, protected by 200 feet of clay and 1,500 feet of granite, picking up natural minerals before it reaches our wells.

Our source and vending machines are licensed by the California Department of Public Health and tested weekly, as state and federal law require. The California Legislature designated the well a California Historic Site, and the North County Times called our water "Eden in a Glass."

History

The well that named a city

Carlsbad takes its name from this water. Its minerals matched the famous spa waters of Karlsbad, Bohemia, now Karlovy Vary in the Czech Republic.

  1. 1882
    Capt. John Frazier strikes water

    Drilling a well for his farm near the beach, Frazier finds mineral water. Labs in New York and Chicago find it chemically similar to the spa waters of Karlsbad, Bohemia.

  2. 1880s
    A town and a Victorian hotel

    Frazier plats his farm as a city named Carlsbad, bottles "The American Carlsbad Mineral Water" for the nation, and builds a hotel by the well. Visitors include at least two U.S. presidents.

  3. 1930s
    Lost in the Depression

    Without funds for upkeep, the owners abandon the well.

  4. 1955
    Rediscovered

    Kay and "Chris" Christiansen find the old well and dream of reviving it.

  5. 1991
    A son of Karlovy Vary arrives

    Kay meets Ludvik Grigoras, born in Karlovy Vary and an émigré to the U.S. at 18. The State of California dedicates the site as a historic landmark.

  6. 1996
    Flowing again after 60 years

    Ludvik and Veronica Grigoras restore and re-drill the wells from 1993 to 1996. Officials from Carlsbad and its new sister city Karlovy Vary attend the re-opening.

  7. 2020
    A third historic well

    Drilling completes in December, securing supply for years to come.
